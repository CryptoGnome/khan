# meme_culture — a memecoin is a joke first and a trade second

The analytical trap: deriving token ideas from news theses alone produces
"correct" concepts nobody laughs at. A thesis explains why a token COULD
move; culture is why anyone actually buys, reposts, and holds a meme. Both
are required — this skill covers the half that spreadsheets miss.

## The bar: would a stranger repeat it?
- The test for a concept is not "is this angle smart?" but **"would someone
  who doesn't hold it screenshot this and send it to a friend?"** If the
  name+image+first-line don't work as a joke on their own, the thesis
  underneath doesn't matter.
- Funny has shapes: absurdist mascots, deadpan literalism, self-aware loser
  energy, over-the-top seriousness about something trivial. Study what shape
  is CURRENTLY winning; it rotates.
- Clever wordplay that needs the news explained first is a losing shape —
  if the joke requires a paragraph, it is a whitepaper.

## Watch the meta, never guess it
The meme meta rotates in days. What worked last month reads as stale — and
launching into a dead shape marks the account as a tourist.
- **Keep a standing read on what is graduating and running**: the launchpad's
  own trending/graduated feeds are ground truth for which *shapes* (mascots?
  text-only tickers? spoofs? animals? inside jokes about the chain itself?)
  are being bought TODAY. Read them for pattern, not for tokens to copy.
- **Track the culture firehose where it lives**: crypto X/timeline humor,
  the launchpad's social floor, what the big meme accounts are posting and
  what actually gets reposted. Free routes first (web, mirrors, trend pages);
  paid reads only when a decision hangs on it.
- **Log the meta read**: each scan cycle records what shapes are hot, what
  is fading, and one line on why. A meta read older than ~48h is expired.
- Being EARLY to a joke is the whole edge: the first token to name a thing
  everyone is about to joke about wins; the fifth is exit liquidity. If the
  timeline is already saturated with the joke, the window closed.

## Derivative launches: copy the moment, never the token
Launching the same ticker/name as a runner is a knife-fight with snipers and
insiders. The winning derivative move is the NEXT joke the runner implies —
the reaction to it, the sequel, the opposite — while the attention wave is
still rising.

## Fuse humor to the thesis
The strongest launches have both: a real catalyst (an event, a narrative)
WEARING a genuinely funny costume. Work order: thesis identifies the wave,
then the concept must independently pass the stranger-repeat test as pure
humor. A thesis-only launch and a joke-only launch both underperform the
fused one.

## Taste rules (the negatives)
- Punching down, tragedy-mining, or edgelord shock = account poison; the
  meme space punishes cringe harder than boredom.
- Trying too hard reads instantly. If the joke needs three exclamation
  points, it isn't landing.
- The launch copy and the account voice carry the joke DRY. The image and
  name do the comedy; the caption underplays it.
